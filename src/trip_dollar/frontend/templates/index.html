Add every participant under “Who’s coming?” first. Codes are single letters, not names or numbers, and are case-sensitive: C and c are different people. Use a decimal point for amounts, such as 12.50; no thousands separators or currency symbols.

Choose who shares it

B12B paid 12. The people ticked for “Default split” share it equally—not necessarily B.
L100CADAL paid 100 CAD. A means everyone on the participant list shares equally.
C500LC paid 500. L bears the whole cost.
C500LCC paid 500. L and C share 250 each.

List each person once. LL is not a weighted split, and AL is invalid: use A alone for everyone. Unlabelled examples above inherit the currency.

Label currency changes

B20CAD L30ABoth expenses are in CAD. The second inherits the first record’s currency.
B20CAD L30USDA C10LCThe first expense is CAD; the next two are USD.
C500LC CADCurrency can come before or after the allocation: this is the same as C500CADLC.

Your first expense needs a currency label unless you set “Starting currency.” “Settle in” only chooses the output currency; it does not label your input. Amounts, merchants and dates never determine currency.

If a group of letters looks like a currency, write C500LBC CAD to make the split clear. Ambiguous input stops the calculation; we don’t guess.

One line or several

C500CADLCL40ATwo adjacent expenses: C paid 500 for L/C; L paid 40 for everyone. Spaces work too.

Dates use MMDD on their own line: 0808 is August 8. Following expenses keep that date until the next heading. Raw headings like 08/08 or 2026-08-08 aren’t supported. Dates are optional; expenses before the first heading have no date.

For online FX, the title supplies the year and the latest MMDD heading supplies the trip’s last date. Without those, or for a trip crossing New Year, set “Rate date” explicitly. Currency always follows the order you wrote the records, never calendar order.

Currency & exchange rates +

Supply your own rates, or leave them blank for online lookup using the trip’s last date. Each currency in your notes needs a rate to the settlement currency. The audit shows the rates and dates actually used.

1 unit on the left equals the amount on the right. Either direction works; one side must be your settlement currency. Separate multiple rates with spaces or new lines. These placeholders are examples, not current rates.

Leave blank to infer from the year title and MMDD headings. On weekends or holidays, lookup uses the latest published rate within the preceding seven days. Future dates need manual rates.

Required only when the first expense has no currency label. A later explicit label changes the currency for following expenses.
